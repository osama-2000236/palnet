## Executive summary

Baydar has a strong change-development foundation: protected-branch conventions, PR CI, lint/type/test/build/E2E checks, staging before manual production promotion, configuration validation, detailed deployment/rollback documentation, and durable handoff/decision records (`.github/workflows/ci.yml:23-193`; `.github/workflows/deploy.yml:39-259`; `docs/deployment.md:106-197`; `CONTRIBUTING.md:12-60`). The principal OPS exposure is the gap between those development controls and verified operations: the current deployment workflow does not check service health after deployment or automate rollback, on-call and status-page ownership remain placeholders, and operational alert/dashboard/incident processes cannot be verified. Observability code is partial: API structured logs and mobile Sentry/PostHog exist, but end-to-end telemetry and its operational use are not established.

**Question score sum: 21 / 55.** Eight prioritized findings: **0 Critical, 1 High, 6 Moderate, 1 Unknown** (unknown is an evidence gap, not a safe rating).

## Architecture and operating-model context

- Current documented path: Next.js on Vercel, NestJS on Render, Neon Postgres via Prisma, R2 media, Expo/EAS mobile, SSE, and external Sentry/PostHog hooks (`docs/deployment.md:5-17,57-104`).
- Operating model: documented as a solo builder with AI pair-programmers, deliberately using managed services without a platform team (`CONTRIBUTING.md:1-3`; `docs/deployment.md:1-3`). Actual staffing, support coverage, and escalation arrangements are unknown.
