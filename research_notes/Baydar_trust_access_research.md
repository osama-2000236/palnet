# Baydar — Palestinian trust, privacy, access, and app-platform research

**Research cut-off:** 13 November 2026  
**Intended use:** evidence notes for a later report writer; not a release approval, compliance certification, or implementation specification.  
**Scope:** a Palestinian professional network with profiles, recruitment, applications, messaging, and user-generated content (UGC), on web and potentially Android/iOS. Payments are conditional, not an assumed Baydar feature.

> **This is research, not legal advice.** The English explanations below are cautious paraphrases of Arabic legal texts, not authoritative translations. Qualified Palestinian counsel must apply the official texts to Baydar's entity, users, data flows, vendors, geography, and launch date.

## 1. How to read these notes

Each substantive statement is labelled:

- **[Legal requirement]** — a duty or prohibition found in the cited legal text. Applicability and interpretation may still require counsel.
- **[Platform requirement]** — an Apple or Google distribution rule; it is not a Palestinian statute.
- **[Standard/best practice]** — a technical standard or proposed product/control response, not automatically a legal duty.
- **[Unresolved question]** — an issue that was not established by the reviewed sources and must not be converted into a requirement by assumption.

**Date convention:** “accessed 13 November 2026” identifies a live-source snapshot. Store policy pages did not expose a useful document-wide version/effective date in the retrieved text, so the access date—not an invented policy date—is used. The legal analysis does not establish territorial enforcement practice or the interaction of Palestinian law with other countries' laws.

## 2. Highest-priority findings

1. **[Legal requirement — not yet operative at cut-off]** Presidential Decree-Law **No. 24 of 2026 on Personal Data Protection** (“PDPL”) was issued on **12 October 2026** and published in Official Gazette **243 on 27 October 2026**. Article 35 says it operates after 30 days from publication; ordinary calendar arithmetic gives **26 November 2026**, which was still future on this note's 13 November research date. [PDPL, metadata and Official Gazette PDF, arts. 32 and 35](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/)
2. **[Legal requirement — separate corrective period]** PDPL Article 32 separately requires entities within scope to rectify their status within **12 months of publication**, with Cabinet authority to extend for a similar period. It does **not** say that the whole law starts after 12 months; do not substitute Article 32 for Article 35. [PDPL, arts. 32 and 35](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/)
3. **[Legal requirement]** The PDPL has broad in-State and extraterritorial scope, covers controllers, processors, and data recipients, and introduces processing bases, notices, data-subject rights, deletion, security, breach, governance, transfer, and enforcement duties. Article 18 delegates the general breach-notification procedures, conditions, and periods to the Authority; it supplies **no single fixed general deadline** in the enacted text. [PDPL, arts. 1–2, 5–26](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/)
4. **[Legal requirement]** For a deletion request within Article 15(1), Article 15(2) gives the controller **seven days from request** and requires immediate notice to data recipients; Article 15(3) gives the recipient **seven days from receiving that notice**. Statutory retention periods and Article 15(4)'s exceptions qualify the right. [PDPL, art. 15](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/)
5. **[Platform requirement]** Independently of the PDPL commencement date, an app distributed through Google Play that supports account creation must provide in-app and web-based deletion initiation and address associated data/service providers; an iOS app supporting account creation must offer account deletion within the app. [Google Account Deletion](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111?hl=en); [Apple App Review Guideline 5.1.1(v)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#data-collection-and-storage)
6. **[Platform requirement]** Profiles, posts, comments, messages, and other UGC trigger store moderation controls, including rules, reporting, blocking where relevant, and moderation/remediation processes. [Google UGC policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9876937?hl=en); [Apple App Review Guideline 1.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#user-generated-content)
7. **[Legal requirement]** Labour Law No. 7 of 2000 Article 2 states equal opportunity without discrimination; Article 16 prohibits discrimination in working conditions. This is relevant to recruitment design, but this review did not establish the complete law of pre-employment advertising, screening, exceptions, or platform liability. [Labour Law No. 7 of 2000, arts. 2 and 16](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/1/)
8. **[Standard/best practice]** WCAG 2.2 AA is a sensible product target, not a verified Palestinian statutory incorporation. The reviewed sources establish Palestine's CRPD obligations concerning ICT accessibility and accessibility in employment, but **no binding Palestinian web/mobile WCAG incorporation was verified in the sources reviewed**. That is a scoped research result, not proof that no such rule exists. [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/); [CRPD arts. 9 and 27](https://www.ohchr.org/en/instruments-mechanisms/instruments/convention-rights-persons-disabilities)
9. **[Platform requirement — conditional]** Neither Apple nor Google billing should be assumed necessary merely because Baydar may later charge. Digital in-app features generally trigger store billing; physical/outside-app services and specified peer-to-peer cases have different treatment. Alternative/external billing depends on the exact feature, storefront, eligibility, and programme/entitlement. [Google Payments](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738?hl=en); [Apple App Review Guidelines 3.1.1–3.1.3](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#payments)

## 4. Privacy and data protection

### 4.1 Scope, roles, principles, and lawful processing

| Classification | Evidence | Baydar implication / boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **[Legal requirement]** | PDPL Article 1 defines personal data broadly as information through which a natural person can be directly or indirectly identified; sensitive personal data includes origin/race, political opinions/affiliations, religious beliefs, health, biometric, and genetic data. “Processing” includes collection, storage, use, disclosure, transfer, erasure, copying, and analysis. [PDPL, art. 1](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/) | Profiles, CVs, identifiers, contact details, messages, device/account logs, and behavioural analysis need a data-map assessment; do not assume pseudonymous analytics are outside the law. Sensitive-data classification depends on content and identifiability, not merely a field label. |
| **[Legal requirement]** | Article 2 applies to controllers, recipients, and processors inside the State regardless of processing location, and to those outside where the data subject resides in the State or processing concerns goods/services to a resident. It excludes competent authorities' processing for crime investigation/prosecution. [PDPL, art. 2](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/) | Baydar should assess itself and each cloud, analytics, communications, recruitment, and support vendor as controller, processor, or recipient. The exclusion is not a general platform exemption. |
| **[Legal requirement]** | Article 5 requires fair, transparent, lawful processing; specified purpose; data minimization; accuracy; storage minimization; reliability/confidentiality; and proportionality to legitimate purpose. [PDPL, art. 5](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/) | Set a purpose, fields, access audience, and retention basis for each data category before collection. “Public professional profile” is not a licence for unlimited reuse. |
| **[Legal requirement]** | Article 6 lists processing bases, including consent; lawfully publicly available data; legislation, legal or contractual obligations, or judgments; specified public/essential/emergency/health/research uses; legitimate interest proven before processing; and permanently anonymized data. [PDPL, arts. 1 and 6](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/) | Record a defensible basis per purpose. Do not treat consent as the only basis or “legitimate interest” as an undocumented catch-all; Article 6(10) requires proof before processing. Article 1 defines anonymization as permanent removal of direct and indirect identifiers preventing re-identification, and Article 14 imposes related controller duties. |
| **[Legal requirement]** | Article 11 requires a lawful, clear, specific purpose; processing within consent/purpose and limited to necessary relevant data; accurate data; lawful means; no harm to the data subject; and technical/organizational measures for security, confidentiality, integrity, and preventing alteration. [PDPL, art. 11](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/) | Reuse of profile/application/message data for ranking, advertising, model training, or a new commercial purpose needs a fresh legal assessment, not implied approval from account creation. |
| **[Legal requirement]** | Council of Ministers Decision 3/2019 Article 1 requires prior permission before companies/institutions providing services make commercial use of direct or indirect personal data of service recipients that they hold. [Decision 3/2019, art. 1](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/247/) | **[Unresolved question]** Counsel must identify the permission route and reconcile this decision with the PDPL before selling/sharing data, lead generation, targeted advertising, or other commercial secondary use. This note does not infer that every paid subscription is “commercial use of personal data.” |
| **[Legal requirement]** | Basic Law Article 32 protects private life and provides that homes may not be entered/searched except by judicial order, and that interference with private life is punishable; it also protects confidentiality of correspondence and communications except as provided by law. [Amended Basic Law, art. 32](https://www.palestinianbasiclaw.org/basic-law/2003-amended-basic-law) | Constitutional/privacy context; it does not supply Baydar's detailed retention schedule or replace the PDPL's operational duties. |

### 4.2 Notice, consent, automated processing, and children

- **[Legal requirement — prior notice]** PDPL Article 12 requires notice before processing of controller details; DPO name/contact if any; purpose and legal basis; data-subject rights; automated processing if used; processing partners if any; recipient information for transfers/exchanges; and source if data was not obtained from the subject. Notice must be clear, understandable, concise, and written or electronic, subject to the prosecutorial exception. [PDPL, art. 12](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/)
- **[Legal requirement — rights transparency]** Article 7(1)(a) includes knowledge of data types, legal basis, purpose, retention period, and decisions based on automated processing including profiling. [PDPL, art. 7](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/)
- **[Legal requirement — consent]** When relying on consent, Article 10 requires clear, explicit, unambiguous, freely given, purpose-specific consent, documented in accessible written/electronic form and given before processing; deceptive or misleading consent is not recognized. Article 9 permits withdrawal/modification at any time by written/electronic notice, requires implementation within **three business days**, preserves prior lawful processing, and—where possible—requires informing recipients within **one business day after implementation** and asking them not to process the data. [PDPL, arts. 9–10](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/)
- **[Legal requirement — automated decisions]** Article 7(1)(z) protects against decisions producing legal effects based on automated processing including profiling. Article 8 allows such processing for obligations under a contract with the data subject, where special legislation allows it, or with consent, and requires safeguards protecting the subject's rights. Article 20(1)(w) also lists processing through automated means among DPO-appointment triggers. [PDPL, arts. 7, 8 and 20](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/)
- **[Legal requirement — minors/incapacity]** Article 9(1) requires consent for a minor or person lacking capacity to come from the legal representative, whose status the controller must verify. Article 3(4) requires the Authority to prepare proposed controls/standards/conditions/safeguards for sensitive and children's data for Cabinet approval. [PDPL, arts. 3 and 9](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/)
- **[Unresolved question]** The reviewed PDPL text did not provide a simple numerical age threshold for an independent child account or prove that Baydar's intended audience is adults only. Confirm the target age audience and obtain the approved children's-data controls before allowing underage registration; do not borrow COPPA/GDPR ages as Palestinian law. [PDPL, arts. 3 and 9](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/)

**[Standard/best practice]** Maintain separate, purpose-specific choices for optional analytics, marketing, public discoverability, contact sharing, and other nonessential uses. Keep the consent record (wording/version, purpose, time, actor, and withdrawal), but do not force consent to unnecessary processing as a condition of core job search. This is a proposed implementation of [PDPL Articles 5, 6, 9–12](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/) and the store minimization rules below, not a claim that a particular UI is prescribed.

### 4.3 Governance, processors, transfers, and assessment

| Classification | Evidence | Baydar implication / boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **[Legal requirement]** | Article 16 requires compliant mechanisms, privacy/security by design, risk management, periodic assessments at Authority-set periods, a special sensitive-data register including authorized access and safeguards, processor appointment under Authority rules, an available privacy policy, data classification, minimum collection, annual compliance/risk/protection/breach reports to the Authority, certification if required by instructions, accessible contact/complaint channels, published complaint procedures, and integration of data management into the organization. [PDPL, art. 16](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/) | Assign an accountable owner and maintain a processing inventory, sensitive-data access record, privacy/complaint process, and vendor controls. The annual-report and certification duties should not be hidden in a generic privacy policy. |
| **[Legal requirement]** | Article 17 requires processors to follow the law, controller instructions, and an agreement specifying scope/purpose/duration; implement safeguards; delete or return data when processing ends as agreed; avoid disclosure except as legally permitted; and maintain an Authority-defined processing record. [PDPL, art. 17](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/) | Put those matters into vendor agreements and deletion workflows. A vendor's own policy does not replace Baydar's instructions or responsibility. |
| **[Legal requirement]** | Article 19 makes the controller responsible for data in its custody and compensation in enumerated failure cases, fully responsible to the data subject for processor acts concerning processing, and requires documented allocation of obligations/responsibilities/roles where multiple processors participate, otherwise joint responsibility applies. [PDPL, art. 19](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/) | Map subprocessors and contractual responsibilities; do not promise that outsourcing transfers accountability away from Baydar. |
| **[Legal requirement]** | Article 20 requires a DPO for public controllers; where the controller's main work is processing; financial/health processing; high-risk processing under Authority criteria; large-volume processing under Authority criteria; automated processing; and Authority-specified cases. The DPO may be internal or external, must be qualified, and contact details must be supplied to the Authority. [PDPL, art. 20](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/) | Baydar's core processing and automated systems make DPO analysis a launch priority. Do not wait for a large-user threshold where another enumerated trigger applies. Articles 21–22 describe duties, resources, independence, and non-retaliation protections. |
| **[Legal requirement]** | Article 23 requires a pre-processing data-protection impact assessment in cases determined by the Authority, with specified purpose, necessity/proportionality, risks, and mitigations; periodic review is required when risks or processing materially change. [PDPL, art. 23](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/) | **[Unresolved question]** Obtain the Authority's list/procedures. Do not claim either that every feature needs a DPIA or that none does until those cases are checked. A voluntary assessment for sensitive profiling or large-scale recruitment screening is prudent. |
| **[Legal requirement]** | Article 24 generally conditions data exchange/transfer on documented consent or written Authority permission, legitimate interests of controller and recipient, notice of recipient/purpose, secure transfer, adequate protection no lower than PDPL standards, pre-allocation of obligations, compensation for harm, and non-editable/non-erasable transfer records including data, recipient, purpose, and consent. Article 25 lists exceptions including legal/contractual obligations and specified public, judicial, epidemic/disaster, and financial cases. Article 26 gives recipients the same obligations as controllers. [PDPL, arts. 24–26](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/) | **[Unresolved question]** Review every cross-border hosting/support/analytics route and decide whether the provider is processor, recipient, or both; identify the applicable basis/exception and any Authority permission. Do not describe all offshore processing as prohibited or all processor hosting as exempt without that analysis. |

## 5. Rights, deletion, export, and retention

### 5.1 Statutory rights and exact periods

| Classification | Evidence | Product implication / limitation |
|---|---|---|
| **[Legal requirement]** | PDPL Article 7 provides rights to access and obtain data in organized machine-readable form; correction; restriction; deletion under Article 15; secure machine-readable transfer/copying to another controller or recipient; protection from legal-effect automated decisions; and knowledge of breaches. Controllers must provide means to exercise rights without contractual/financial consequences except an Authority-approved cost of obtaining data, and may request additional identity-verification information. [PDPL, art. 7](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/) | Build authenticated access/export, correction, restriction, portability, deletion, and complaint routes. Use proportionate verification so account recovery does not itself become an excessive data-collection exercise. |
| **[Unresolved question — export timing]** | Article 7 states the access/portability rights but does not set a general fixed number of days for access/export in the text reviewed; the seven-day periods in Article 15 concern qualifying deletion requests, not every rights request. [PDPL, arts. 7 and 15](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/) | Do not invent a universal seven-day export deadline. Set a service target and check Authority instructions, while preserving Article 15's actual deletion periods. |
| **[Legal requirement — deletion trigger]** | Subject to retention periods in other legislation, Article 15(1) permits a complete deletion request when the purpose or lawful basis ends, consent is withdrawn/modified where processing relied on it, or processing contravened the PDPL/subordinate legislation. [PDPL, art. 15(1)](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/) | A single “delete account” action needs purpose-by-purpose handling; account closure does not automatically erase every statutory record. |
| **[Legal requirement — deletion period]** | Article 15(2) requires controller execution within **seven days from request** and immediate notification of the deletion request to recipients; Article 15(3) requires recipients to execute within **seven days from receipt of the controller's notice**. [PDPL, art. 15(2)–(3)](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/) | Timestamp request receipt, identity resolution, deletion completion, recipient notification, and recipient completion. Escalate vendor failures rather than treating an API “accepted” response as erasure. |
| **[Legal requirement — exceptions]** | Article 15(4) excludes deletion requests for specified governmental records; data concerning legal obligations or needed for the controller to claim rights owed by the data subject; statistical/scientific-research data; health data; and criminal-investigation data. [PDPL, art. 15(4)](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/) | Have counsel map each exception and statutory retention period. “Legal obligation” is not a reason to retain the entire profile forever; isolate retained records, restrict use/access, and disclose the basis/duration. |
| **[Legal requirement — processor end]** | Article 17(4) separately requires processors to delete data after the processing period or return it to the controller according to their agreement. [PDPL, art. 17(4)](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/) | Contract deletion/return must include production replicas, exports, support tooling, and subprocessors, subject to lawful retention and verified backup handling. |

### 5.2 Cybercrime retention must be reconciled, not ignored

- **[Legal requirement — scope-dependent]** Cybercrime Decree-Law 10/2018 Article 1 contains a broad “service provider” definition, and Article 31 imposes service-provider duties including retaining subscriber information for **not less than three years**. [Cybercrime Decree-Law, arts. 1 and 31](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/83/)
- **[Unresolved question]** The reviewed material does not establish that every Baydar profile, message, application, CV, or deletion request falls within Article 31, nor exactly which subscriber-information fields and commencement event apply. Counsel must determine Baydar's status, field-level retention scope, lawful access controls, and interaction with PDPL Article 15. Do not respond either by deleting required records or by converting a three-year subscriber-information rule into three-year retention of all content. [Cybercrime Decree-Law, arts. 1 and 31](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/83/); [PDPL, art. 15](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/)
- **[Legal requirement — criminal constraint]** The Cybercrime Decree-Law contains offences involving unlawful access/interference and misuse of systems/data; these criminal-law constraints do not supply the PDPL's controller security programme, breach report, or data-subject rights. [Cybercrime Decree-Law](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/83/); [PDPL, arts. 16–18](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/)

### 5.3 Store deletion requirements apply separately

- **[Platform requirement — Google Play]** Google's Account Deletion guidance requires an app that enables account creation to let users initiate account and associated-data deletion **within the app** and through a **web resource**; the web link is supplied in the Play Console account-deletion declaration and shown to users. Deletion must include associated user data, deletion requests must reach service providers where applicable, and legitimate retention must be disclosed. Temporary deactivation, freezing, or an indefinite account-disable state is not the required deletion path. [Google Account Deletion, accessed 13 November 2026](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111?hl=en)
- **[Platform requirement — Apple]** Apps supporting account creation must offer account deletion within the app; apps without significant account-based features should allow use without login, and personal information may be required only where directly relevant to core functionality or required by law. Social-network credential revocation/data-access disablement must be available in-app where applicable. [Apple Guideline 5.1.1(v), accessed 13 November 2026](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#data-collection-and-storage)
- **[Standard/best practice]** Provide the same account lifecycle on web and mobile: close account, delete associated data, export first, explain retained categories and reason/duration, revoke sessions/tokens, stop nonessential messages, and provide status/help. Do not make users email support when an in-app route is required. Basis: [Google Account Deletion](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111?hl=en), [Apple 5.1.1(v)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#data-collection-and-storage), and [PDPL arts. 7, 15 and 17](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/).

### 5.4 Minimum deletion/retention control set

**[Standard/best practice]** Before launch, create a field-level schedule containing: data object and identifiers; purpose/legal basis; controller/processor/recipient; storage regions; active and backup locations; start event; statutory/contractual retention basis; deletion exception; reviewer; deletion mechanism; recipient propagation; and evidence of completion. The schedule must cover CVs, profile revisions, public posts, private messages, applications, moderation records, verification documents, auth/session records, telemetry, support exports, analytics, and backups. This is a recommended way to reconcile [PDPL Articles 5, 7, 15–17](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/) with [Cybercrime Article 31](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/83/) and store rules; the PDPL does not prescribe this table format.

**[Standard/best practice]** Do not claim “instant permanent erasure” unless all systems actually implement it. Define how active deletion, encrypted/aged backups, replicas, caches, logs, third parties, and legal holds operate; ensure retained backup data is not restored into active use without reapplying deletion, and obtain counsel approval for any backup grace period. The enacted Article 15 text reviewed supplies no general backup-deletion grace period. [PDPL, art. 15](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/)

## 6. Security and breach response

### 6.1 Duties established by the PDPL

- **[Legal requirement]** Article 16 requires technical and organizational security/privacy measures from design through processing, risk management, periodic evaluations, sensitive-data registers/access controls, data classification, and annual compliance/risk/protection/breach reports. Article 11 requires measures ensuring security, confidentiality, integrity, and prevention of alteration. [PDPL, arts. 11 and 16](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/)
- **[Legal requirement]** Article 17 requires processors to apply necessary technical/organizational security and privacy measures, process only under the agreement/instructions, and protect against unauthorized disclosure. [PDPL, art. 17](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/)
- **[Legal requirement — controller]** Article 18(1) requires a controller, upon learning of a breach affecting privacy/confidentiality/security, to take necessary security/technical measures and notify the Authority with investigation results according to Authority-set procedures, conditions, and periods. The notice includes nature/causes, DPO details, possible effects, and taken/proposed mitigations. Article 18(2) requires breach documentation and data-subject notification under Authority-set procedures, conditions, and periods. [PDPL, art. 18(1)–(2)](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/)
- **[Legal requirement — processor]** Article 18(4) requires a processor to notify the controller **immediately upon learning** of a breach so the controller can act and notify the Authority. [PDPL, art. 18(4)](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/)
- **[Legal requirement — subject-notification exceptions]** Article 18(3) says individual notice is not required where a breach targeted a large number of people, contact is impossible, or breached data is protected so it cannot be viewed, subject to Authority instructions. This is not a blanket encryption exemption and not permission to avoid notifying the Authority. [PDPL, art. 18(3)](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/)
- **[Unresolved question — deadline]** The enacted text reviewed does **not** set a general “72-hour,” “24-hour,” or other fixed controller-to-Authority or controller-to-subject deadline. Obtain current Authority instructions and preserve immediate processor escalation and prompt containment. Do not import GDPR's 72-hour rule as if it were the Palestinian text. [PDPL, art. 18](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/)
- **[Legal requirement — consequences]** Articles 19 and 27–29 provide controller liability/compensation mechanisms, corrective measures, fines, and possible judicial destruction/cancellation consequences in the circumstances specified. [PDPL, arts. 19 and 27–29](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/)

### 6.2 Proposed operational baseline

**[Standard/best practice]** Maintain least-privilege access, MFA for privileged accounts, encryption in transit and at rest, managed secrets, environment separation, audit logs, dependency/secret scanning, secure upload handling, rate limits, tested recovery, and vendor/subprocessor review. Define incident severity, on-call escalation, evidence preservation, containment, investigation, recipient/data-subject communications, regulator decision, and post-incident remediation. These are recommended controls derived from the outcomes in [PDPL Articles 11 and 16–19](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/) and [Apple Guideline 1.6](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#data-security), not a claim that the law names these exact technologies.

**[Platform requirement]** Apple Guideline 1.6 requires appropriate security measures for proper handling of user information and prevention of unauthorized third-party use, disclosure, or access; the developer is also responsible for third-party SDK compliance. [Apple Guidelines, introduction and 1.6](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#data-security)

### 7.2 Proposed Baydar operating model

**[Standard/best practice]** Treat job posts, employer profiles, professional profiles, endorsements, comments, attachments, links, and messages as UGC surfaces. Provide per-object reporting, user blocking, clear prohibited-content rules, spam/fraud controls, moderation triage, evidence-preserving case records, notices of action, appeal/escalation, and an auditable response queue. Include image/document/link screening and a manual route for nuanced employment scams, impersonation, threats, harassment, discriminatory content, and nonconsensual disclosure. These controls implement the store outcomes above; the stores do not prescribe Baydar's exact staffing or SLA. [Apple 1.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#user-generated-content); [Google UGC](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9876937?hl=en)

**[Standard/best practice]** Set service targets based on severity and actual staffing, then publish only targets the operation can meet. [Apple 1.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#user-generated-content); [Google UGC](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9876937?hl=en)

**[Legal requirement]** Criminal-law provisions in the Cybercrime Decree-Law may constrain unlawful access, data/system interference, and specified online conduct. [Cybercrime Decree-Law 10/2018](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/83/)

**[Unresolved question]** Counsel should verify applicable unlawful-content categories, court/regulatory orders, notice preservation, intermediary duties, disclosure obligations, lawful process for user records, and any employment-platform-specific rules. No general Palestinian UGC takedown timetable or platform safe harbour was established in this review; that is not a finding that none exists. [Cybercrime Decree-Law 10/2018](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/83/)

**[Unresolved question — deletion versus moderation records]** Define whether reported content, reporter identity, evidence snapshots, and sanctions may be retained under a legal obligation/claim or another basis after account deletion; segregate and restrict such records rather than silently exempting all UGC from deletion. [PDPL, arts. 6, 7 and 15](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/); [Cybercrime Decree-Law, art. 31](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/83/)

## 8. Recruitment equality and nondiscrimination

- **[Legal requirement]** Labour Law 7/2000 Article 2 frames work as a right of every citizen capable of it and requires equal opportunity without discrimination; the State is to provide opportunities on the basis of competence/capability. [Labour Law, art. 2](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/1/)
- **[Legal requirement]** Article 16 prohibits discrimination in working conditions. [Labour Law, art. 16](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/1/)
- **[Legal requirement — disability treaty obligation]** CRPD Article 27 recognizes the right to work on an equal basis in an open, inclusive, and accessible labour market, prohibits disability-based discrimination in employment including recruitment and hiring, and requires reasonable accommodation in the workplace. [CRPD, art. 27](https://www.ohchr.org/en/instruments-mechanisms/instruments/convention-rights-persons-disabilities)
- **[Standard/best practice]** Require objective job-related qualification fields; identify and review protected-characteristic exclusions; provide a route to report discriminatory listings; keep human review/appeal for automated screening; explain ranking/rejection logic where used; and provide accessible application/contact alternatives. Do not infer religion, political opinion, health, disability, or other sensitive traits from profile activity for ad targeting or screening. This is a proposed risk-control response to [Labour Law Articles 2 and 16](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/1/), [CRPD Article 27](https://www.ohchr.org/en/instruments-mechanisms/instruments/convention-rights-persons-disabilities), and [PDPL Articles 1, 6–8 and 11](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/2167/), not a verbatim statutory feature list.
- **[Unresolved question]** Counsel should establish which protected grounds and occupational exceptions apply at job advertisement, applicant screening, interview, offer, and employment stages; employer versus platform responsibility; documentation/appeal expectations; and the domestic implementation of CRPD recruitment duties. The two Labour Law provisions reviewed do not resolve those questions by themselves. [Labour Law, arts. 2 and 16](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/1/); [CRPD, art. 27](https://www.ohchr.org/en/instruments-mechanisms/instruments/convention-rights-persons-disabilities)
- **[Platform requirement]** Apple separately prohibits specified defamatory, discriminatory, or mean-spirited content, and UGC safeguards apply to recruitment content as well as social posts. [Apple 1.1.1 and 1.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#objectionable-content)

## 9. Electronic transactions, contracts, and evidence

- **[Legal requirement — current instrument]** Electronic Transactions and Trust Services Decree-Law 17/2024 Article 63 repeals Decree-Law 15/2017; Article 64 provides operation after 30 days from Official Gazette publication. The 2024 instrument—not the repealed 2017 decree—is the basis for this note. [Electronic Transactions 17/2024, arts. 63–64](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/1784/)
- **[Legal requirement — electronic writing/records]** Article 12 gives electronic writing equivalence to traditional writing where its information is retained for use/reference and the decree's conditions are met. Article 13 specifies record conditions concerning retention/retrieval, preserving original-form information to demonstrate accuracy, and identifying originator/sender/recipient and date/time of sending/receipt. [Electronic Transactions 17/2024, arts. 12–13](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/1784/)
- **[Legal requirement — contracting]** Article 15 permits binding offer/acceptance through data messages when its conditions are met. Article 20 recognizes contracting between electronic agents, and between an electronic agent and a person who knows or should know it will form/perform automatically, without direct natural-person intervention. [Electronic Transactions 17/2024, arts. 15 and 20](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/1784/)
- **[Legal requirement — qualified/trust services]** Articles 26–27 define trust services and prohibit providing them or issuing related certificates without a licence. The decree distinguishes electronic, advanced, and qualified signatures/seals and associated trust-service conditions. [Electronic Transactions 17/2024, arts. 1 and 26–35](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/1784/)
- **[Unresolved question]** Ordinary Baydar click acceptance is not automatically a licensed “trust service,” but neither does Article 20 remove contract-law formalities or prove every signature/record is sufficient for every employment or regulated transaction. Counsel should identify exclusions/formalities and when qualified trust services are required. [Electronic Transactions 17/2024, arts. 15, 20 and 26–35](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/1784/)
- **[Standard/best practice]** For terms, recruiter agreements, and paid orders: show the complete text before acceptance; require affirmative action; preserve terms version, actor/account, timestamp, consent/acceptance event, notices, transaction details, and receipts in retrievable form; prevent silent retroactive edits; and provide downloadable copies. These are evidence/control recommendations based on [Electronic Transactions Articles 12–15 and 20](https://maqam.najah.edu/legislation/1784/), not a declaration that every event needs a qualified signature.

## 10. Accessibility and bidirectional access

### 10.1 Binding versus technical sources

- **[Legal requirement — international obligation]** Palestine acceded to the CRPD on **2 April 2014**. [UN Treaty Collection, CRPD status](https://treaties.un.org/Pages/ViewDetails.aspx?src=TREATY&mtdsg_no=IV-15&chapter=4&clang=_en)
- **[Legal requirement — international obligation]** CRPD Article 9 calls for appropriate measures to ensure access on an equal basis, including to information/communications technologies and systems; Article 21 calls for public information in accessible formats/technologies appropriate to different disabilities, in a timely manner and without additional cost. [CRPD, arts. 9 and 21](https://www.ohchr.org/en/instruments-mechanisms/instruments/convention-rights-persons-disabilities)
- **[Standard/best practice]** WCAG 2.2 is a W3C Recommendation of **12 December 2024**, with testable technology-independent criteria applicable to web content on desktop and mobile. W3C also notes that even full conformance does not address every user need. [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)
- **[Unresolved question]** No binding Palestinian web/mobile incorporation of WCAG 2.0, 2.1, or 2.2, or a particular conformance level, was verified in the reviewed sources. Do not turn that narrow non-verification into “Palestine has no accessibility law.” Obtain any implementing legislation, public/private-sector scope, procurement rules, and enforcement guidance from Palestinian counsel. [CRPD, art. 9](https://www.ohchr.org/en/instruments-mechanisms/instruments/convention-rights-persons-disabilities); [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)

### 10.2 Recommended target

**[Standard/best practice]** Target **WCAG 2.2 AA** across the complete journeys: registration/recovery, profile, job discovery, application, messaging, reporting/blocking, export, deletion, and any payment. Include semantics/names/roles/states, keyboard access and visible focus, contrast, text resize/reflow, meaningful errors, accessible dialogs, target size, alternatives for media/documents, and screen-reader testing in Arabic RTL and mixed Arabic/English content. Automated checks alone are insufficient; test with disabled Palestinian users and common assistive technology. Basis: [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) and [CRPD Articles 9, 21 and 27](https://www.ohchr.org/en/instruments-mechanisms/instruments/convention-rights-persons-disabilities).

**[Standard/best practice]** Use language and base-direction markup, isolate user-generated inline text, and apply directional controls for mixed Arabic/English/numeral strings instead of relying on CSS or Unicode guessing alone. [W3C, “Inline markup and bidirectional text in HTML,” accessed 13 November 2026](https://www.w3.org/International/articles/inline-bidi-markup/)

## 11. Mobile permissions and privacy disclosures

### 11.1 Shared design rules

- **[Platform requirement — Apple]** Apps collecting user/usage data must obtain consent, subject to the guideline's stated lawful-basis proviso; provide understandable withdrawal; write clear purpose strings; minimize data; respect permission settings; and offer alternatives where possible. Apple expressly gives the example of allowing manual address entry when location is denied and using pickers/share sheets instead of full Photos/Contacts access. [Apple 5.1.1(ii)–(iv)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#data-collection-and-storage)
- **[Platform requirement — Apple]** Apps may not require push notifications, location, or tracking to access functionality/content or receive compensation; data collected for one purpose may not be repurposed without further consent unless law explicitly permits it. [Apple 5.1.2(i)–(ii)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#data-use-and-sharing)
- **[Platform requirement — Google Play]** Sensitive permissions must be necessary for current, prominently disclosed core functionality, requested in context through incremental flows, limited to consented purposes, and used with respect for refusal. Apps should not manipulate users into optional permissions and should provide reasonable alternatives where feasible. Use minimum scopes and system pickers where sufficient. [Google Play Permissions and APIs that Access Sensitive Information](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16558241?hl=en); [Google User Data](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311?hl=en)

- **[Platform requirement — privacy policy]** Apple requires a privacy-policy link in App Store Connect and easily accessible within every app, identifying collected data/collection/uses, equivalent third-party protection, retention/deletion, and consent revocation/deletion requests.
